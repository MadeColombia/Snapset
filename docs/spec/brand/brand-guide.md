# Snapset brand guide

Task D2 groundwork. This page says who Snapset is, how it sounds, how it looks and how it shows up in people's lives. It builds on [principles.md](../principles.md) (how the app behaves) and the kettlebell app icon in [logo/](../logo/) (chosen 2026-10-31). The visual board is the [Snapset Brand Board](https://claude.ai/artifact/MTipQFgxkuv9TK4Ka71q37) (source: brand-board.html in this folder).

Status: **approved by MadeColombia on 2026-10-31.** This is the base for every design, copy and marketing decision in Snapset. If something needs to break a rule here, change this guide first (with a dated note) rather than making a one-off exception.

---

## 1. Who we are for

**The person:** someone who has decided to take the gym seriously, but is still new to it. They have maybe tried a few months on and off, followed a video or two, and downloaded a gym app that greeted them with a dashboard full of charts, percentages and terms they did not know. They closed it and went back to guessing.

What they feel walking in:

| They feel | What they need from us |
|---|---|
| "Everyone here knows what they're doing except me." | Quiet confidence. A plan that already exists, so they never stand there wondering. |
| "I don't know if I'm doing this right." | Show how (demo loop on every exercise) and tell them they're on track. |
| "There's too much information." | One thing at a time. The next set, nothing else, unless they ask. |
| "I'll probably quit again." | Progress they can see in plain numbers, and a warm welcome back after a gap, never guilt. |

**Who it is not for (and that's fine):** competitive powerlifters, people who want every metric, people who want a social feed. They are well served elsewhere. Saying no to them keeps Snapset simple.

> Note: the "Who it is for" section in principles.md described people who "already know the exercises". That was written for experienced lifters, so it has been updated to match this audience (2026-10-31).

## 2. The brand idea

**Snapset is your spotter.**

A good spotter stands behind you, says very little, and is there when you need them. They don't lecture, they don't shout, they don't film you. They tell you "you've got this, two more" and they catch the bar if it goes wrong.

That is the whole personality in one picture. Every word, colour and animation can be checked against it: *would a calm, kind spotter do this?*

**Promise:** *Know what to lift next.* Open the app and the next set is already there: the exercise, the weight, the reps. Snap a photo or tap Save. That's it.

**Tagline:** *Know what to lift next.* (approved with the guide; the others are kept as alternatives)

1. **Know what to lift next.** (recommended: it is the promise, in five plain words)
2. One set at a time.
3. The gym, made simple.

## 3. Personality

| We are | So we... | We are not | So we never... |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calm** | show one thing at a time, use dark quiet screens, move slowly | Hype | shout, use "BEAST MODE", flashing confetti, countdown pressure |
| **Clear** | use everyday words, give the number first | Technical | lead with jargon (1RM, RPE, hypertrophy) or charts nobody asked for |
| **Encouraging** | notice effort and progress, welcome people back | Preachy | guilt, streak shaming, body talk, diet advice |
| **Steady** | keep the same layout every session, save instantly | Flashy | redesign the screen per mood, hide things behind gestures |
| **Honest** | say how sure the photo reading is, explain every target in one line | Magic | pretend the AI is perfect, make medical claims |

## 4. Voice and tone

The **voice** never changes: a calm, friendly spotter who uses plain words. The **tone** changes with the moment.

### Voice rules

1. **Number first, reason second.** "40 kg × 10. Same as last time, you got all 10."
2. **Short sentences.** If a line wraps on an iPhone SE, cut it.
3. **Plain words.** Say "the most you can lift once", not "1RM". If a gym term is truly needed, explain it the first time it appears.
4. **Talk to one person.** "You", never "users" or "athletes".
5. **Praise the action, not the body.** "You added 2.5 kg this week." Never "You're getting shredded."
6. **No guilt, ever.** No "You missed 3 days!", no broken-streak warnings, no sad faces.
7. **No exclamation marks except for real wins** (a new best). One per screen at most.
8. **Sentence case** for everything: buttons, titles, tabs. "Start workout", not "Start Workout".
9. **No em dashes** in app copy. Use a full stop or a comma.

### Tone by moment

| Moment | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| First open, onboarding | Welcoming, reassuring | "Let's set up your first week. You can skip any question." |
| Mid-workout, between sets | Minimal, almost silent | "Next: 40 kg × 10" |
| Photo reading unsure | Honest, easy fix | "Not sure about this one. Is it 40 kg?" |
| Exercise done | Quiet nod | "Done. 3 sets of bench press." |
| New personal best | Warm, a real cheer (the one place for "!") | "New best: 50 kg on the squat!" |
| Back after a break | Kind, practical | "Welcome back. We've made today a bit lighter so you can ease in." |
| Something went wrong | Calm, says what to do | "Couldn't read the photo. Enter it by hand, it's already filled in." |
| Paywall | Plain, no pressure | "Snapset Pro reads your weights from a photo and plans with you. Try it free for 7 days." *(trial length is a placeholder until pricing is decided)* |

### Words we use and avoid

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| set, reps, weight | volume, load, intensity | Everyone understands the first three |
| the most you can lift once | 1RM | Jargon |
| next time | progressive overload | Show the idea, don't name it |
| a bit lighter / heavier | deload, overload | Plain |
| how hard it felt (easy, okay, hard) | RPE 7 | Beginners cannot rate RPE reliably |
| day off, rest day | missed day | Rest is part of training |
| welcome back | you lost your streak | No guilt |
| warm-up, cool-down | mobility protocol | Plain |
| best | PR, PB | "PR" is fine as a secondary label once people know it |
| coach (Pro only) | AI, assistant, bot | P10: the AI is a coach, never a chat |

### Rewrites from the current flows

| Before (flows.md) | After |
|---|---|
| "hit 12 on all sets last time, +2.5 kg" | "You got 12 on every set last time. Add 2.5 kg." |
| "Weights 10% lighter after 12 days off" | "Welcome back. Weights are 10% lighter today after 12 days off." |
| "No pulling exercise yet" | "Add a pulling exercise to balance this routine." |
| "Harder than your level" | "A step up from your level. You can still try it." |
| "Will sync when you're online" | (keep, it is already right) |

## 5. Colour

The palette comes straight from the app icon: cast-iron graphite with a pin-orange accent and chalk white. It feels like a real gym (iron, chalk, the orange pin in the weight stack) and stays calm because almost everything is grey.

### Core colours

| Name | Hex (dark) | Hex (light) | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graphite | `#14171B` | `#F7F5F0` (Chalk paper) | Background |
| Iron | `#1E2227` | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, sheets |
| Iron raised | `#2A2F36` | `#ECE8DF` | Steppers, chips, pressed states |
| Iron line | `#3A3F47` | `#D9D4C9` | Dividers, outlines, kettlebell in the icon |
| Chalk | `#F3F0E8` | `#14171B` | Main text, the big numbers |
| Chalk muted | `#A9ADB4` | `#5B616B` | Secondary text |
| **Pin orange** | `#FF5A2C` | `#C9400F` | The one accent: primary button or coach card |

### Status colours (always with an icon or word, P8)

| Name | Dark | Light | Meaning | Icon (SF Symbol) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sage | `#6FCF97` | `#1F7A4D` | Going up, new best, saved | `arrow.up` / `checkmark` |
| Sky | `#7DB3E8` | `#2F64A8` | Hold, information | `equal` / `info.circle` |
| Amber | `#F2B84B` | `#8A5A00` | Going lighter, needs a look, couldn't read | `arrow.down` / `exclamationmark.circle` |

**There is no red in Snapset.** Nothing a beginner does in the gym is a failure. A lighter target, a low-confidence reading or a failed save are all amber with a clear next step. The only exception is iOS's own destructive buttons (Delete account), which keep the system red so they look the same as every other app.

### Rules

1. **One orange thing per screen.** Either the primary button or the coach card, never both competing. This is the visual version of P10.
2. **Text on orange is Graphite, not white.** Graphite on Pin orange is 5.8:1; white or chalk on Pin orange is about 2.7:1 and fails. In light mode, the deeper orange `#C9400F` takes white text (5.0:1).
3. **Orange text is for large text and icons only on raised surfaces.** Pin orange on Iron raised is 4.3:1, which passes for text 18 pt and larger but not for body text.
4. **Grey does the work.** 90% of any screen is Graphite, Iron and Chalk. If a screen looks colourful, something is wrong.

Contrast ratios checked with the WCAG 2.1 formula (script in this thread). All text pairs in the tables above are 4.5:1 or better on their own background, except where rule 3 says otherwise.

## 6. Typography

**Typeface: SF Pro, the iOS system font.** It is free, already on every iPhone, supports Dynamic Type, and makes Snapset feel native and trustworthy. No custom font to license or load.

**Numbers use SF Pro Rounded with monospaced digits.** Weights and reps are the biggest thing on screen (P2). Rounded figures feel friendlier and less clinical, and monospaced digits stop the number jumping when it changes from 8 to 10.

In SwiftUI (verify in Apple's current docs, these are standard modifiers):

```swift
Text("40")
    .font(.system(size: 64, weight: .semibold, design: .rounded))
    .monospacedDigit()
```

### Type scale (use the Dynamic Type styles, never fixed sizes for text)

| Role | SwiftUI style | Weight | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero number | custom 64 pt, scales with Dynamic Type via `@ScaledMetric` | Semibold, rounded | **40** kg |
| Screen title | `.largeTitle` | Bold | Today |
| Section title | `.title3` | Semibold | Bench press |
| Body | `.body` | Regular | You got 12 on every set last time. |
| Secondary | `.subheadline` | Regular, Chalk muted | Last time: 37.5 kg × 12 |
| Label, chip | `.footnote` | Medium | Warm-up |

Two weights per screen at most (Regular plus Semibold or Bold). Bold is for titles and numbers, never for whole sentences.

## 7. Iconography and imagery

**Icons:** SF Symbols only, in the regular or medium weight, so they match the text and adapt to Dynamic Type and Bold Text automatically. One accent icon per screen at most.

**Exercise demos (Task 22):** a single neutral figure, flat Iron grey on Graphite, with the working muscle in Pin orange. No faces, no gendered body type, no muscles drawn as "goals". The figure shows *how*, not *what you should look like*.

**Photography (App Store, website, social):**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Ordinary people in ordinary gyms, mid-rest, phone in hand | Shirtless models, before and after bodies |
| Close-ups of real equipment: the weight-stack pin, chalk, plates, the kettlebell | Stock "fitness" photos with perfect lighting and fake sweat |
| Warm, dim gym light with one orange detail | Neon, red, aggressive colour grading |
| People of different ages, sizes and backgrounds | One body type |

**The icon is the logo.** The kettlebell with the lens says both halves of the product (lifting + snap a photo). In marketing, the word mark is "Snapset" in SF Pro Rounded Bold, with only the S capitalised, next to the icon. There is no mascot: the cartoon kettlebell was tried and rejected on 2026-10-31.

## 8. Motion and feel

Motion should feel like a calm breath, not a firework.

- **Springs settle, they don't bounce.** Use `.smooth` or `.snappy` springs with no visible overshoot. (Verify these presets in Apple's current SwiftUI docs.)
- **Fast:** 0.2 to 0.35 s for most transitions. Nothing makes the lifter wait.
- **Saving a set:** a light haptic and the row settling into the list. That's the reward.
- **New best:** the one celebration. The number gets a Sage glow and a single success haptic. No confetti.
- **Reduce Motion:** fades instead of movement, still frames instead of demo loops (P12).

## 9. How we connect with people

The brand is mostly felt in a few moments. These are where we spend our care.

| Moment | What we do | Why it builds trust |
|---|---|---|
| **App Store page** | First screenshot: the workout screen with one big number. Headline "Know what to lift next." No feature grid. | The page itself shows we are the simple one |
| **First workout** | A ready routine waits after onboarding; first set already filled in; a line under the demo: "First time? Watch the loop, then go light." | Removes the "standing there lost" fear |
| **Between sets** | Almost nothing. Rest timer, next number. | Respect for their focus |
| **Session summary** | Facts in plain words: "3 exercises, 9 sets. Next time: squat goes up 2.5 kg." | Shows progress without charts |
| **After a gap** | "Welcome back" plus lighter weights. Never a count of missed days. | The moment most apps lose beginners |
| **Notifications** | Opt-in, at most one reminder on planned training days, written like a friend: "Leg day is ready when you are." Never "Don't lose your streak!" | Notifications are a privilege |
| **Monthly look back** | One short card in Home: "This month: 11 workouts, bench up 5 kg." | Progress is the best motivation for beginners |
| **Support and reviews** | Reply in the same voice: short, warm, specific. | The brand is the same everywhere |

### Ideas worth considering (not decided)

- **Plain-words glossary:** tap any gym word in the app (superset, warm-up set) for a one-line explanation. Fits the audience strongly; needs a task in todo.md if wanted.
- **"First month" path:** a ready-made 4-week beginner routine library section with lighter starting weights.

## 10. Quick checklist for any new screen or text

- [ ] Would a calm, kind spotter say or show this?
- [ ] Is there only one orange thing?
- [ ] Is the number the biggest thing, with a plain reason next to it?
- [ ] Any jargon? Replace it or explain it.
- [ ] Any guilt, pressure or red? Remove it.
- [ ] Does colour have an icon or word with it?
- [ ] Sentence case, no em dashes, at most one "!"?
